How to Use the Senior School Timetable

Read these rules before setting up subjects or generating the timetable.

1. Full Elective Simultaneous Blocks (3 Option Lines)

Every learner selects one subject from each Option Line (three electives in total). All subjects listed inside a single Option Line are taught at the exact same time in different classrooms or workshops. Each option line occupies 5 lessons per week.

Timetable BlockWeekly LessonsComplete List of Subjects Taught Simultaneously
Option Line 15 lessonsPhysics, General Science, History and Citizenship, Literature in English, Fasihi ya Kiswahili, Indigenous Languages, Sign Language (Elective), Fine Arts, Woodwork, Metalwork, Building Construction, Marine and Fisheries Technology
Option Line 25 lessonsChemistry, Geography, Business Studies, Home Science, Electricity, Power Mechanics, Aviation, Sports and Recreation, Theatre and Film
Option Line 35 lessonsBiology, Agriculture, Computer Studies, Media Technology, Christian Religious Education (CRE), Islamic Religious Education (IRE), Hindu Religious Education (HRE), Arabic, French, German, Mandarin Chinese, Music and Dance

2. Why These Subjects Are Grouped Together

  • Pure Sciences Separated: Physics (Line 1), Chemistry (Line 2), and Biology (Line 3) sit in separate lines so STEM learners can take all three or any pair.
  • Humanities Separated: History (Line 1), Geography (Line 2), and CRE/IRE/HRE (Line 3) sit in separate lines so Social Science learners can combine them freely.
  • Religious & Foreign Languages Grouped: CRE, IRE, and HRE share Line 3 because students only take one religion, and Arabic, French, German, and Mandarin share Line 3 because students rarely take two foreign languages simultaneously.
  • Technical & Arts Balanced: Practical workshops (Woodwork, Electricity, Aviation, Home Science) and creative arts (Fine Arts, Theatre, Music) are spread across all three lines so Arts and Technical learners can build full 3-subject combinations.

3. Non-Blocked Homeroom Lessons (15 Lessons/Week)

The remaining 15 lessons do not require cross-stream blocking and should be slotted per individual class stream:

  • English: 5 lessons per week
  • Community Service Learning (CSL): 3 lessons per week
  • Physical Education (PE): 3 lessons per week
  • ICT Skills: 2 lessons per week
  • Pastoral Programme Instruction (PPI): 1 lesson per week
  • Personal / Group Study: 1 lesson per week
Important setup instructions
  1. In the Senior School elective strokes section, place each offered elective in its correct Option Line. Do not put the same subject in more than one line.
  2. Assign a responsible qualified teacher to every elective offered by the school and enter its approved weekly lesson count. Different subjects in the same stroke may have different weekly counts.
  3. Subjects in one Option Line share the same scheduled block window. Each subject receives only its own assigned lessons; any remaining block periods are shown as supervised guided-study / elective-project activities.
  4. Set up the non-blocked subjects for each class stream using the weekly lesson counts above.
  5. Check teacher clashes, class lesson loads and all validation messages before accepting the generated timetable.

Lesson-count note: The figures supplied in this guide account for 15 elective lessons plus 15 non-blocked lessons (30 lessons total). They do not specify the other 10 lessons in a 40-period week; configure those separately according to the school's approved curriculum and timetable requirements.

STEP-BY-STEP WORKFLOW

From First Setup to a Completed Timetable

Follow these steps in order. Complete the setup and resolve validation messages before accepting or printing a timetable.

Recommended order: School profile → Grades → Daily structure → Teachers → Subjects and teacher assignments → Senior School combinations and rooms (where applicable) → Elective strokes → Scheduling rules → Generate → Validate → Back up and print.

STEP 1 — Sign in and confirm school access

  1. Log in using the school administrator email and password used when the school account was registered.
  2. Check the school name shown at the top of the application.
  3. If an approval or payment banner is displayed, read its instructions. Setup may be available while generation, printing or PDF export remains locked until the required approval is completed.
  4. Do not share the administrator password with teachers or other users.

STEP 2 — Complete the school profile and logo

  1. Open 5. School Profile & Logo.
  2. Confirm the official school name exactly as it should appear on printed timetables.
  3. Upload the school logo in PNG, JPG or WEBP format.
  4. Click Save school profile and confirm that the preview and saved status are correct.

STEP 3 — Add every grade and stream

  1. Open 1. Setup and find 1. Grades and streams.
  2. Enter one grade or stream name at a time, then click Add grade. Examples: Grade 10, 9 Red, 7A or 8 East.
  3. Add all streams that need their own class timetable. Use consistent names because the same names will be used when assigning subjects and viewing results.
  4. Tick the grades/streams that should be included in generation. Check the selection again before generating a selected-grades timetable.

STEP 4 — Set the school day, breaks and activities

  1. In Setup, set the school starting time, lesson duration and number of lessons per day.
  2. Set the short health-break duration and preferred first and later break times.
  3. Set lunch duration, the lesson after which lunch should occur, and an optional preferred lunch start time.
  4. Set games duration and whether games should occur before the first lesson or after the last lesson. Add a preferred games start time if required.
  5. Review the displayed timetable notes, including any grade-specific timing requirements. Make sure the chosen number of periods and day structure reflect the school's real programme.

STEP 5 — Register all teachers

  1. Go to Teachers, subjects and grade assignments.
  2. Enter each teacher's full name in Register a teacher.
  3. Enter the subject or subjects the teacher is qualified to teach in the teaching-subjects field, then click Register teacher.
  4. Repeat until all available teachers have been registered. Use consistent spelling of names and subject titles.
  5. Register teachers before assigning subjects so that they appear in the teacher-selection list. Do not register the same teacher multiple times to represent different subjects.

STEP 6 — Add subjects and assign a teacher to each grade

  1. Choose the target Grade / Stream from the assignment dropdown.
  2. Review the subject picker. If a required subject is missing, type its name (or names separated by commas) in Add subject(s) not listed and add it to the list.
  3. Select the subjects that this grade studies. Click Assign selected subjects.
  4. For each subject in the assignment sequence, choose the correct registered teacher and enter the required Lessons per week. Save the assignment and continue through the remaining selected subjects.
  5. Repeat for every grade/stream. A subject taught in multiple grades needs a separate grade assignment for each grade, with the correct teacher and weekly lesson count.
  6. Review the assignment table. Confirm that no subject is missing a teacher, no teacher is attached to the wrong grade, and the weekly lesson counts are correct.
  7. If a teacher changes, select the subject again and use the reassignment flow to choose the replacement teacher. Remove incorrect assignments only after confirming the replacement plan.

STEP 7 — Configure Senior School combinations and resources (if used)

  1. For Senior School, open Senior School: Pathways, combinations & elective blocks.
  2. Where the school uses named combinations/streams, select the grade, enter the combination name and complete the available combination details, including estimated enrolment and subject choices as requested by the form.
  3. Register the classrooms, laboratories, workshops or specialist spaces the school will use. Give each room a clear name, correct room type and realistic maximum capacity.
  4. Check that laboratory-based or practical subjects have an appropriate compatible room and that the room capacity can accommodate the combined learners taking that lesson.
  5. Repeat for every combination and room required by the school. Do not create combinations or rooms that the school does not actually use.

STEP 8 — Configure the three Senior School elective strokes

  1. Open 2. Scheduling Rules and find Senior School elective strokes — exactly THREE.
  2. Place each elective offered by the school into the correct Stroke A, Stroke B or Stroke C. A learner's three-elective choice can be one subject from each stroke; the timetable lines also support combinations such as three choices from one pathway or two from one pathway and one from another, provided the selected subjects are distributed across the three simultaneous option lines.
  3. Subjects in the same stroke are taught at the same time. A learner should choose no more than one subject from each stroke.
  4. Do not place the same elective in more than one stroke. Remove electives the school does not offer from the active stroke arrangement.
  5. For every elective included in a stroke, confirm that its qualified teacher assignment exists and its own approved weekly lesson count is correct. Select subjects requiring a consecutive double in Scheduling Rules → Double lessons. Check that one teacher is not assigned to two subjects that would run simultaneously in the same stroke.
  6. Read the stroke status and correct any warnings before generating.

STEP 9 — Set the scheduling rules

  1. In Double lessons, select the subjects that require a consecutive double lesson each week. Enable the strict double rule if that is the school's requirement.
  2. In Morning priority, select subjects that should be prioritised earlier in the day and confirm the morning cutoff.
  3. Review Daily subject frequency. The application applies its displayed weekly-load and daily-frequency rules; check any overrides carefully.
  4. Review the Senior School strokes again and confirm that the three blocks are correct.
  5. Return to Setup and, if required, select teachers and weekdays for Teacher afternoon release. Only select release days that reflect the actual staffing arrangement.

STEP 10 — Perform a complete pre-generation check

  1. Confirm that every grade/stream has been added and selected where required.
  2. Confirm that every subject offered to each grade has a teacher and the correct weekly lesson count.
  3. Check teacher qualifications and availability. Resolve duplicate, missing or incorrectly assigned teacher records.
  4. Check that the daily timetable, breaks, lunch, games and special scheduling rules are correct.
  5. For Senior School, check all three strokes, combination details, enrolments, rooms, laboratory compatibility and capacities.
  6. Read all status messages in Setup and Scheduling Rules. Fix errors and warnings before proceeding.

STEP 11 — Generate the timetable

  1. Open 3. Generate & View, then go to Generate and inspect.
  2. Choose the required mode: Master Whole-School Timetable for the coordinated school timetable; Selected Grades Timetable for selected grades; Individual Teacher Timetable to inspect one teacher; or Senior Combination / Stream Timetable to inspect one Senior School combination.
  3. For Selected Grades, select the grade/stream to view. For Individual Teacher, select the teacher. For Senior Combination, select the combination. Choose a day or Full week as required.
  4. Review the teacher workload preview and confirm that the totals look reasonable.
  5. Click Generate and allow the optimisation process to finish. Do not refresh or close the page while generation is running.

STEP 12 — Inspect, validate and correct the result

  1. Inspect the master timetable and then open individual grade, teacher and combination views. Confirm that lessons appear in the expected days and periods.
  2. Check that all assigned lessons have been placed, teachers are not double-booked, and Senior School blocks are synchronised as intended.
  3. Open Management & Analytics and run the available independent checks and certification controls.
  4. If the application reports unplaced lessons, teacher clashes, room/capacity conflicts or rule violations, return to the relevant setup area, correct the underlying assignment or rule, and generate again.
  5. Do not treat a timetable as final while validation reports unresolved errors. Manual timetable edits should be followed by the independent checks again.

STEP 13 — Save a backup and publish the approved timetable

  1. Open 4. Backup & Restore and create/download a backup after completing setup and again after approving the final timetable.
  2. In Management & Analytics, save a named timetable version before major changes so that you can identify and restore a checkpoint if needed.
  3. Use the teacher, grade, master or combination view to make a final visual review.
  4. Use Print, Export CSV or Save / Print PDF as appropriate. Printing/PDF may require the school's account approval.
  5. Keep the backup file in a safe school location. This release stores timetable setup in the current browser; use the backup/restore process when moving to another browser or device.
Important: A successful generation message is not a substitute for checking the timetable. The school should verify lesson coverage, teacher availability, room suitability, elective combinations and all certification results before publishing the timetable to learners and staff.

GRADE 10 · PRACTICAL TIMETABLING

💡 Pro Tips: Planning Weekly Double Lessons

Guidance for allocating uninterrupted practical, workshop, fieldwork and studio time.

22 Grade 10 subjects require double lessons (80 minutes each) per week to give learners enough uninterrupted time for laboratory experiments, workshop fabrication, field projects, or studio rehearsals. For a standard 5-lesson subject, allocate 1 double lesson (2 periods) + 3 single lessons (3 periods) per week.

1. Science & Applied STEM Subjects (8 Subjects)

These require setup, practical experiments, specimen observation, or farm fieldwork:

  • Biology (1 double + 3 singles)
  • Chemistry (1 double + 3 singles)
  • Physics (1 double + 3 singles)
  • General Science (1 double + 3 singles)
  • Agriculture (1 double + 3 singles)
  • Home Science (1 double + 3 singles)
  • Computer Studies (1 double + 3 singles)
  • Media Technology (1 double + 3 singles)

2. Technical & Engineering Subjects (7 Subjects)

Workshop-based subjects need continuous blocks for machine setup, safety checks, and tool cleanup:

  • Aviation (1 double + 3 singles)
  • Building Construction (1 double + 3 singles)
  • Electricity (1 double + 3 singles)
  • Metalwork (1 double + 3 singles)
  • Power Mechanics (1 double + 3 singles)
  • Woodwork (1 double + 3 singles)
  • Marine and Fisheries Technology (1 double + 3 singles)

3. Arts & Sports Science Subjects (4 Subjects)

Creative and physical electives need extended time for studio work, rehearsals, and warm-up/cool-down routines:

  • Fine Arts (1 double + 3 singles)
  • Music and Dance (1 double + 3 singles)
  • Theatre and Film (1 double + 3 singles)
  • Sports and Recreation (1 double + 3 singles)

4. Compulsory / Support Areas (3 Subjects)

Even though these have fewer total lessons per week, scheduling a double allows practical execution:

  • Community Service Learning (CSL) – 3 lessons/week (1 double + 1 single for field/community activities)
  • Physical Education (PE) – 3 lessons/week (1 double + 1 single to allow changing into sports kit and playing full sessions)
  • ICT Skills – 2 lessons/week (1 double per week for hands-on computer lab practice)

Quick Tip for Plotting Doubles on Your Timetable

  • Pair Doubles Within the Same Option Line: Since all subjects in Option Line 1 run at the exact same time, schedule Option Line 1's double lesson on the same day (for example, Tuesday Lessons 3 & 4) so Physics, Woodwork, Fine Arts, and General Science get their double simultaneously while History or Literature teachers use those two periods for extended writing or seminar work.
  • Best Time Slots: Place double lessons right before break, before lunch, or during the last two lessons of the afternoon so practical cleanup does not delay the next class.

How RASM 360 handles mixed subjects in one elective stroke

  • Double-required subjects: A selected subject receives one genuine consecutive pair, followed by its remaining single lessons. The same qualified teacher and compatible room are reserved for both periods of the double.
  • Subjects with a lower weekly allocation: The subject receives exactly its assigned number of lessons. If the shared stroke has additional periods, the learner remains in the block for a supervised Guided Independent Study / Elective Project activity linked to that subject. The subject teacher supervises it and the appropriate room capacity is checked.
  • Five single lessons alongside a double: All five single periods are preserved. The two periods occupied by the other subject's double remain two separate single lessons for this subject; they are not labelled as a double. Language adjacency rules still apply and may make some mixed arrangements incompatible.
Implementation reminder: Use the Double Lessons settings to select the subjects that need consecutive periods, then verify that the generated timetable places each double in adjacent periods and that no teacher, learner combination, or specialist room is double-booked. Review the guided-study / project periods in each combination timetable and teacher timetable before publishing.

🎓 Senior School: Pathways, combinations & elective blocks

Register only the subject combinations or streams that the school offers for Grades 10–12; this does not create learner records. For a simple school-wide option structure, use the three elective strokes in Scheduling Rules: one subject is selected from each stroke, with five simultaneous lessons per stroke. This optional combinations area remains available for schools that need to record named school-offered combinations and estimated enrolments.

🏫 Senior School rooms & laboratories

Register rooms before generating Senior School blocks. Capacity is checked against the combined enrolment of all combinations taking the same subject in that block. Laboratory subjects require a compatible specialist room.

Automatic elective block allocation

A conflict is created when two subjects appear together in at least one registered combination. The engine uses three-colour graph assignment with saturation-based ordering, forward checking and backtracking. Conflicting subjects cannot share a block.

Add combinations, then analyse the subject conflicts.

Senior School certification: every combination must receive 40 occupied periods (8 per day × 5 days). Blocks A, B and C run in shared weekly sessions across all combinations; subjects in the same block must have matching weekly lesson counts. Every Senior assignment must have a recorded matching teacher qualification; all core and elective sessions must have an available room of the correct type and sufficient capacity. Any missing lesson, teacher clash, qualification mismatch, room clash or capacity failure prevents certification.

Special scheduling rules

Choose subjects that should be prioritised early in the day and subjects that must be taught in two consecutive lessons. Double lessons can start at any valid consecutive pair from Lesson 1 through the final lesson pair of the day; they are balanced across the week rather than restricted to morning periods. Subjects with related concepts or skills must not follow one another in consecutive periods across any grade. This includes language-to-language lessons, related sciences and Mathematics with Physics. Creative Arts receives a strong preference for the period immediately before a break or lunch, without making otherwise valid timetables impossible. The solver also balances each grade’s morning and afternoon lesson load across the week.

4. Senior School elective strokes — exactly THREE

Subjects in the same stroke are taught simultaneously.

Three option blocks only: Stroke A, Stroke B and Stroke C. A learner selects one subject from each stroke. Each elective keeps its own weekly lesson count and its own double/single setting. For example, Chemistry can be set to Requires one double while CRE in the same stroke remains Singles only. The double is shared as a simultaneous stroke time-slot, but CRE is still recorded as two separate single lessons in those periods. A double uses two of the existing 40 weekly periods; it does not add periods. Any unused periods for a lower-load subject become supervised guided-study / elective-project activities. The generator checks that each Senior combination receives exactly 40 occupied periods (8 per day × 5 days), and checks teacher and room clashes.
